Orakl · decision simulator

A second opinion that disagrees with itself first

Describe what you are weighing. Orakl runs a swarm of simulated agents holding deliberately opposing views, lets them argue, and hands you the structured analysis — not a one-word answer.

The method

  1. You state the decision in your own words“Should I take the offer or stay?” — plain language, with as much or as little context as you want to add.
  2. The swarm is seeded with opposing priorsAgents are not identical. Some are given reasons to favour the move, others reasons to resist it, so the argument is real rather than a chorus.
  3. They argue, and the disagreement is measuredHow split the swarm is matters as much as where it lands. A 51/49 result is reported as one, not rounded into false confidence.
  4. You get the reasoning, not a verdictThe strongest case for and the strongest case against, the factors that moved the result, and the confidence — so you can disagree with it on specific grounds.

A high split with high confidence is Orakl telling you the decision is genuinely close and that it knows why — which is more useful than a number pretending to be certain.

It never leaves your device

The whole simulation runs locally, on the iPhone or the Mac in front of you. There is no account, no server and no upload — which is the only reason it is reasonable to type a real decision about your job, your money or your family into it.

Honest limits

Orakl simulates argument; it does not know anything about your specific circumstances beyond what you type, and it cannot see the future. It is a structured way to think, in the tradition of writing down both columns before you decide — not a forecast, and not advice of any professional kind.
